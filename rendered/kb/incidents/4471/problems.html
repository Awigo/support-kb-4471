owner Awigo open reviewed 2026-10-29 · 0 days ago

#4471 is marked known: one issue, one fix queued. It is not one issue. The desk merged every report whose subject matched (decision 0001), and the subjects say nothing about the cause: the same few subject lines come in for every problem below. Read by tenant, booking form, channel and what each report says happened, the reports split into separate problems, each with its own ticket and its own fix.

The queued parser fix does not close #4471. It leaves the form-hint reports and the one-day shifts open, and nobody is working on them.

Counts come from reports-4471.json at render time, one per report id: the export lists some reports twice. Every report is counted where its tenant files it, the desk’s own view. The one-day shifts are filed under the parser, so they are counted there and again on their own row.

How to tell them apart

  • parser: day and month swapped (booked 4 July, invite says 7 April). Through the app, email, or the booking-v2 form, which tells people to type the day first. The shared parser reads every date month first. Fix: parse in the tenant’s locale order.
  • hint: the same swap, but only through the booking-v3 form of a day-first tenant. That form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field; people type what it says, and the form reads it day first. Fixing the parser does not fix this one. Fix: print the hint in the tenant’s order.
  • one-day shift: the date moves by one day, not a swap (12 May shows as 11 May; Lisbon gets Tuesday, Toronto gets Monday). The desk had already noted these as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) but filed them under the parser. Most likely a time zone conversion; not confirmed.
  • unplaced (only shown when there are any): a tenant not on file, so the locale and form are unknown. Read the report before filing it.

The intake tag on a report is a guess from the reporter’s browser, not the cause. It disagrees with the tenant’s cause often enough that it should not be used to sort these reports.

What the desk should change

  • Merge by cause, not by subject. The dedupe explorer already shows the difference: the same subject can be two causes, and two subjects can be one.
  • Before marking a big ticket known, read a handful of bodies from each tenant and each channel.
